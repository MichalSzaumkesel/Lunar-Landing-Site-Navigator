You are a Sonnet teammate on scope B. Read `tasks/_common.md`, `tasks/RULES-quality.md`, `tasks/UI-guidelines.md` first. Writable paths: `src/viz3d/**` (code, css, tests) only. Do not change the store, the contract, data files, or other components. Do not commit anything.

## Context

The 3D tab (`src/viz3d/SurfaceView.tsx`, `scene.ts`, `lander.ts`, `skyBodies.ts`, `terrain.ts`, `surface.css`) shows the terrain patch, a lander at the origin, the Sun and Earth as sky bodies synced to the time cursor, a star field, and controls: "On the surface", "Overview", "Face the Sun", "Vertical exaggeration" slider, a "Magnify Sun and Earth x4" checkbox, "Sunlight map", "Find sunniest spot", lander position control. The user reports: (1) Face the Sun often points at a Sun that sits just above or below the horizon and is barely visible; (2) magnify should be a slider like vertical exaggeration, (3) there is no "Face the Earth"; (4) the Sun, Earth, lander and the lunar surface look flat and basic, and should look like 3D renders from a good game. The scene is currently dark and low contrast (see it yourself, below).

## Items

1. **Face the Earth** next to "Face the Sun", same behaviour and style. Both buttons aim the camera azimuth at the body and set the pitch so the body is clearly in frame: pitch = max(body elevation, 4 degrees) minus a small offset so the body sits around one third from the top of the view, never looking below the horizon line; when the body is below the horizon, keep the azimuth, show it in a small text note in the view ("Sun is 3.2 deg below the horizon") and draw a soft glow at the horizon in that direction so the user understands why nothing is visible. Animate the move (about 0.8 s, ease in-out) using the existing `cameraTo` API (cancelled by user input, jump when `prefers-reduced-motion`). Buttons disabled with a title when the body direction is not available.
2. **Size sliders.** Replace the "Magnify Sun and Earth x4" checkbox by one slider "Sun and Earth size" from 1x (true angular size) to 20x, step 0.5, default 1x, same look as "Vertical exaggeration" (label, value readout, `aria-valuetext` like "4 times true size"). It scales the rendered angular radius of both bodies about their direction. Keep the state in the component; no store change. Show "True size" in the readout at 1x. Everything stays labelled approximate where it already is.
3. **Sun.** A photographic look: a very bright core with limb darkening, a corona/glow (additive sprite or shader, soft falloff, scales with the size slider), subtle bloom or glare (use `three/examples/jsm` postprocessing only if it keeps 60 fps on an integrated GPU, otherwise a cheap glow sprite), a faint horizontal-ish lens glare optional. When the Sun is partly behind terrain it must clip correctly (depth test on the terrain). Light the scene from the Sun's true direction with hard shadows (existing shadow map), high contrast, warm-white colour.
4. **Earth.** A convincing planet sphere that is lit from the Sun direction so its phase matches the real phase (the "Earth lit %" readout is the reference; verify they agree by looking at crescent and gibbous dates), ocean specular glint, a procedural cloud layer, a thin atmosphere rim (fresnel shader), and a soft night side with a little earthshine-style ambient. No downloaded textures and no network requests: generate textures procedurally on a canvas at startup (under 150 ms). Continents are a stylised procedural pattern, not geography: add the comment `decorative, not geographic` in code and keep the readout wording factual (only phase and elevation are data). Make it crisp at the maximum slider size.
5. **Lunar surface.** PBR-style material for the terrain: regolith colour variation, fine procedural normal or bump detail at two scales (tileable canvas textures, triplanar or world-space UVs so there is no stretching on slopes), small craters and scattered rocks as instanced meshes placed deterministically (seeded random, a few thousand instances max, with shadows only on the nearer ones), slight darkening and haze toward the horizon, filmic tone mapping (ACES) and correct sRGB output, a faint earthshine fill so shadows are not pure black, hard soft-edged sun shadows (PCF soft). Keep the terrain geometry from the data untouched (decoration is shading only; the horizon silhouette must stay data-true). Keep the sunlight heatmap overlay working.
6. **Lander.** Replace the box-like model by a detailed procedural lander built from three.js primitives and extrusions: octagonal or hexagonal body with gold and silver foil materials (metalness, crinkle bump), four splayed landing legs with struts and footpads (pads sink slightly into the regolith with a small dust-ring decal), engine bell under the body, an antenna dish on a mast, small thruster blocks, a ladder or hatch detail, a flag-less neutral look. No branding, no real mission markings (it is a generic lander). Scale in metres consistent with the scene (a small lander, roughly 4 m across including legs); it must still be placed on the surface and aligned to the local normal as today, and stay selectable for the move feature.
7. **Sky.** Better stars (magnitude-based sizes and colours, procedural, deterministic) and a faint Milky Way band texture; pure black near the Sun glow falloff only. No claims of real star positions (decorative; comment it).
8. **Quality guard.** Add a small quality tier (high, medium, low) chosen automatically from measured frame time over the first 2 s (and overridable with `?q=low` in the URL hash or query), which lowers shadow map size, instance counts, texture sizes and disables bloom. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any ambient animation (there should be almost none). Dispose every geometry, texture, material and render target on unmount; no memory growth when switching the 3D tab on and off ten times (check `renderer.info.memory` in a test or a console script).
9. **Tests.** Pure helpers (pitch computation for Face the Sun/Earth including the below-horizon case, slider value to angular radius, quality tier choice, seeded rock placement determinism and count caps, Earth phase fraction from the Sun and Earth directions vs the "Earth lit %" formula) get unit tests. Do not test pixels.
